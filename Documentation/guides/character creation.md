# Character Creation Guide / პერსონაჟის შექმნის გზამკვლევი

This guide is a step-by-step walkthrough for building a character in this homebrew Almerion project.

It follows the order in `rules/character creation.md` and provides all the concrete rules you need:
* what options you can choose from
* the exact bonuses and effects of each choice
* why the decision matters
* what information you should write down on the sheet

**All campaigns in this project start at Level 1.** This guide assumes you are building a level 1 character.

## Before you start

Have these things ready:
* a blank character sheet or notes page
* the equipment list from `rules/equipment and items.md`
* your campaign or GM notes, if the table uses house limits

## 1) Start with a character concept

Before selecting mechanics, decide what kind of character you want to play.

This is not a mechanical choice, but it makes every later choice easier.

### Ask yourself
* Are they a hero, a survivor, a mercenary, a scholar, or a criminal?
* Are they brave, cautious, cruel, helpful, sarcastic, or disciplined?
* Do they work best in melee, at range, with magic, through stealth, or through support?
* Are they a lone wanderer or part of a faction, order, clan, or military unit?

### Concept options you can start from
* frontline warrior
* agile scout
* arcane caster
* divine healer
* wilderness tracker
* social manipulator
* utility specialist

### Why this matters
Your concept helps you decide which race, class, and background make sense.

## 2) Choose your race

Race defines your character's physical identity, cultural identity, and some mechanical strengths.

Race provides:
* fixed ability bonuses (listed below for each race)
* special traits
* senses like darkvision
* movement speed
* languages
* visual and cultural identity

### Available race options with exact bonuses

#### Human
* Ability Bonuses: +1 to any two abilities (your choice)
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 30 ft
* Languages: Common + one extra language of your choice
* Traits: Adaptable, versatile, extra utility

#### Elf
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Dexterity, +1 Intelligence or Wisdom (your choice)
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 30 ft
* Languages: Common, Elvish
* Traits: Darkvision, keen senses, trance, charm resistance

#### Dwarf
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Constitution, +1 Strength or Wisdom (your choice)
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 25 ft
* Languages: Common, Dwarvish
* Traits: Darkvision, poison resistance, toughness, stone-craft knowledge

#### Halfling
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Dexterity, +1 Charisma or Constitution (your choice)
* Size: Small
* Speed: 25 ft
* Languages: Common, Halfling
* Traits: Lucky, brave, nimble

#### Gnome
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Intelligence, +1 Dexterity or Wisdom (your choice)
* Size: Small
* Speed: 25 ft
* Languages: Common, Gnomish
* Traits: Darkvision, cleverness, tinkering tendencies

#### Dragonborn
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Strength, +1 Charisma
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 30 ft
* Languages: Common, Draconic
* Traits: Breath weapon, draconic ancestry

#### Half-Elf
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Charisma, +1 to any two abilities (your choice)
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 30 ft
* Languages: Common, Elvish, + one extra language of your choice
* Traits: Darkvision, adaptability, skill versatility

#### Half-Orc
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Strength, +1 Constitution
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 30 ft
* Languages: Common, Orc
* Traits: Darkvision, relentless endurance, intimidation

#### Tiefling
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Charisma, +1 Intelligence or Dexterity (your choice)
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 30 ft
* Languages: Common, Infernal
* Traits: Darkvision, fire resistance, infernal heritage

#### Aasimar
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Charisma, +1 Wisdom or Constitution (your choice)
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 30 ft
* Languages: Common, Celestial
* Traits: Radiant heritage, healing/holy resilience

#### Orc
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Strength, +1 Constitution
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 30 ft
* Languages: Common, Orc

#### Elementborn
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Constitution, +1 to an elemental ability of your choice
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 30 ft
* Languages: Common, Primordial

#### Goliath
* Ability Bonuses: +2 Strength, +1 Constitution
* Size: Medium
* Speed: 30 ft
* Languages: Common, Giant
* Traits: Powerful build, cold resistance, mountain endurance

### What you are deciding here
You are deciding what kind of being your character is and what natural strengths they start with.

### Why it matters
Race directly adds to your ability scores and gives you special traits that affect how you interact with the world. Racial bonuses are applied after Point Buy and add to your final scores.

### What to write down now
* race name
* racial language(s)
* size
* speed
* which optional ability bonuses you chose, if any
* racial traits and any resistances or darkvision
* which extra language you took, if applicable

## 3) Generate ability scores with Point Buy

This project uses Point Buy only.

You do not roll for ability scores. Instead, you build them by spending a limited pool of points.

### Basic Point Buy flow
1. Start every ability at 8.
2. You have 27 points to spend.
3. Spend those points to raise the six abilities.
4. **Maximum score before racial bonuses is 15. Minimum is 8.**
5. Higher scores cost more points, so specialization has a price.

### Point Buy cost table
This is the official cost to raise a score by one level:
* 8 to 9 = 1 point
* 9 to 10 = 1 point
* 10 to 11 = 1 point
* 11 to 12 = 1 point
* 12 to 13 = 1 point
* 13 to 14 = 2 points
* 14 to 15 = 2 points

### The six abilities and what they mean
* Strength — lifting, striking, grappling, heavy physical force
* Dexterity — speed, aiming, dodging, stealth, finesse
* Constitution — endurance, hit points, resistance to fatigue and pain
* Intelligence — memory, learning, analysis, technical knowledge
* Wisdom — perception, insight, instinct, spiritual or practical awareness
* Charisma — presence, leadership, confidence, social force

### Example Point Buy arrays for different builds
* Strength-based martial (Fighter, Barbarian): 15 STR, 12 DEX, 14 CON, 8 INT, 10 WIS, 10 CHA (costs: 7+1+4+0+0+0=12 points; 15 remaining)
* Dexterity-based (Rogue, Ranger): 12 STR, 15 DEX, 13 CON, 10 INT, 12 WIS, 10 CHA (costs: 1+7+2+0+1+0=11 points; 16 remaining)
* Spellcaster Intelligence (Wizard): 10 STR, 12 DEX, 13 CON, 15 INT, 11 WIS, 10 CHA (costs: 0+1+2+7+0+0=10 points; 17 remaining)

### How to approach Point Buy
Choose scores based on your role and class:
* martial character → prioritize Strength or Dexterity, then Constitution
* spellcaster → prioritize your class casting stat, then Constitution
* scout → prioritize Dexterity, then Wisdom or Constitution
* social character → prioritize Charisma, then Constitution or Dexterity

### Common Point Buy strategy
* Put your main stat at 15 (costs 7 points).
* Make your secondary stat 12-14.
* Keep Constitution respectable (13+).
* Leave your dump stat at 8 if your concept allows it.

### Why this matters
Ability scores are the foundation for: attack rolls, saving throws, skill checks, spellcasting, initiative, and later character progression.

## 4) Translate scores into modifiers

Once your Point Buy scores are chosen, convert each one into its modifier.

### Modifier formula
`(Ability Score - 10) / 2`, rounded down

### Examples
* 8 or 9 → -1
* 10 or 11 → +0
* 12 or 13 → +1
* 14 or 15 → +2
* 16 or 17 → +3
* 18 or 19 → +4
* 20 or 21 → +5

### Record these immediately
* Strength modifier
* Dexterity modifier
* Constitution modifier
* Intelligence modifier
* Wisdom modifier
* Charisma modifier

These modifiers will be used constantly during play.

## 5) Apply racial bonuses and choose optional racial abilities

Now add your race's fixed ability bonuses to your Point Buy scores.

Your final ability scores = Point Buy score + Racial bonus.

For races that give you a choice (like Elf's +1 to Int or Wis), decide now.

### Example
* You bought 15 Strength with Point Buy.
* You chose Elf, which adds +2 Dexterity and +1 Wisdom.
* Your final Dexterity is 10 + 2 = 12.
* Your final Wisdom is 10 + 1 = 11.
* Your final Strength remains 15.

### Recalculate modifiers if changed
If your bonuses push a score up, recalculate that ability's modifier.

### Write down
* final ability scores after racial bonuses
* new modifiers, if any changed
* racial traits and resistances
* racial senses like darkvision

## 6) Choose your class

Your class is the biggest mechanical choice.

It determines your: primary combat role, Hit Dice and starting HP, proficiencies, class features, resource system (if any), and long-term leveling path.

### Available classes with detailed descriptions

#### Barbarian — The Tank/Damage Dealer
* Hit Dice: d12 (highest hit points per level)
* Primary Abilities: Strength, Constitution
* Combat Role: Front-line fighter, takes heavy damage, deals heavy damage, naturally defensive
* Playstyle: Activate Rage to become extremely durable and aggressive; excel at melee combat and soaking damage; be nearly unkillable
* Best for: Players who want to absorb punishment and deal consistent damage
* Proficiencies: All simple and martial weapons, light and medium armor, shields
* Starting Resource: Rage (limited uses per rest, grants bonus damage and damage resistance)

#### Bard — The Support/Utility Specialist
* Hit Dice: d8
* Primary Abilities: Charisma, Dexterity
* Combat Role: Support, control, skill expert, flexible caster
* Playstyle: Enhance allies with spells and bardic magic; control the field with crowd control; be the face of the party in social situations; heal and buff with spells
* Best for: Players who want to help others and solve problems in creative ways
* Proficiencies: Light armor, simple weapons, some musical instruments
* Starting Resource: Spell slots, Bardic Inspiration (bonus dice to help allies)

#### Cleric — The Healer/Support
* Hit Dice: d8
* Primary Abilities: Wisdom, Constitution
* Combat Role: Healer, support, light melee
* Playstyle: Cast divine spells to heal, protect, and damage enemies; wear light to medium armor; balance healing with offensive magic; smite evil
* Best for: Players who want to keep the party alive and smite enemies with holy power
* Proficiencies: Light and medium armor, shields, simple weapons
* Starting Resource: Spell slots

#### Druid — The Nature Specialist
* Hit Dice: d8
* Primary Abilities: Wisdom, Constitution
* Combat Role: Control, support, nature magic, occasional damage
* Playstyle: Cast nature spells for control and utility; transform into animals for scouting or combat; heal when needed; command the battlefield with nature
* Best for: Players who love wilderness themes, animals, and flexible magic
* Proficiencies: Light and medium armor, shields, druidic weapons
* Starting Resource: Spell slots, Wild Shape (later levels)

#### Fighter — The Weapon Master
* Hit Dice: d10 (second-highest HP)
* Primary Abilities: Strength or Dexterity, Constitution
* Combat Role: Reliable damage, sustained combat performance, weapon expertise
* Playstyle: Master your chosen weapons and armor; fight reliably turn after turn; develop a signature fighting style; be the most consistent damage dealer
* Best for: Players who want straightforward, effective combat without complexity
* Proficiencies: All simple and martial weapons, all armor, shields
* Starting Resource: Fighting Style (choose bonus), Second Wind (self-healing)

#### Monk — The Mobile Striker
* Hit Dice: d8
* Primary Abilities: Dexterity, Wisdom
* Combat Role: Damage, mobility, control, speed
* Playstyle: Fight unarmed or with martial arts weapons; move quickly around the battlefield; use Ki for special abilities and bonus attacks; be a whirlwind of strikes
* Best for: Players who want fast, technical combat and unarmed mastery
* Proficiencies: Simple weapons, certain martial arts weapons; NO heavy armor
* Starting Resource: Ki points (fuel special abilities), Unarmored Defense

#### Paladin — The Holy Warrior
* Hit Dice: d10 (second-highest HP)
* Primary Abilities: Strength, Charisma
* Combat Role: Armored fighter, burst damage with smite, support
* Playstyle: Wear heavy armor and fight in melee; activate divine smites to deal big damage with your attacks; use spells for protection and healing; heal and protect allies
* Best for: Players who want armor, melee combat, and holy power combined
* Proficiencies: All simple and martial weapons, all armor, shields
* Starting Resource: Spell slots, Divine Smite (burst damage mechanic)

#### Ranger — The Scout/Tracker
* Hit Dice: d10 (second-highest HP)
* Primary Abilities: Dexterity, Wisdom
* Combat Role: Ranged damage, wilderness utility, scouting
* Playstyle: Fight with bow or light weapons; track and scout wilderness; use spells for utility; work best in your favored terrain; blend combat and exploration
* Best for: Players who want ranged combat and exploration focus
* Proficiencies: All simple and martial weapons, light and medium armor, shields
* Starting Resource: Spell slots, Favored Enemy

#### Rogue — The Skill Master/Precision Striker
* Hit Dice: d8
* Primary Abilities: Dexterity, Intelligence or Charisma
* Combat Role: Precision damage through stealth, skill master, tool expert
* Playstyle: Deal massive damage with sneak attacks when you have advantage or an ally near the target; pick locks, disarm traps, and gather information; specialize in a skill; be invaluable in social and exploration
* Best for: Players who love stealth, locks, infiltration, and being the party's skill expert
* Proficiencies: Simple weapons (most), light armor, thieves' tools
* Starting Resource: Sneak Attack (bonus damage mechanic), Expertise (doubled proficiency bonus)

#### Sorcerer — The Natural Caster
* Hit Dice: d6 (lowest hit points, most fragile)
* Primary Abilities: Charisma, Constitution
* Combat Role: Damage, control, flexible spell-casting
* Playstyle: Cast spells with natural magic power; modify spells with Sorcery Points for flexibility; be mobile and devastating from range; adapt spells on the fly
* Best for: Players who want raw magical power and flexibility in spell-casting
* Proficiencies: Simple weapons, light armor options vary by class
* Starting Resource: Spell slots, Sorcery Points (modify spells)

#### Warlock — The Pact Caster
* Hit Dice: d8
* Primary Abilities: Charisma, Constitution
* Combat Role: Damage, control, unique magical abilities
* Playstyle: Form a pact with a powerful entity for magic; cast repeatable spells through Invocations; be powerful but different from other casters; blend martial and magical combat
* Best for: Players who want a unique magical identity and flavor different from other casters
* Proficiencies: Simple weapons, light armor
* Starting Resource: Spell slots, Pact features, Invocations

#### Wizard — The Prepared Caster
* Hit Dice: d6 (lowest hit points, most fragile)
* Primary Abilities: Intelligence, Constitution
* Combat Role: Control, utility, knowledge
* Playstyle: Prepare spells each day from a broad list; control the battlefield with magic; use rituals for utility outside combat; be the most versatile caster
* Best for: Players who love variety, control, and planning ahead
* Proficiencies: Simple weapons, light armor limited
* Starting Resource: Spell slots, Spellbook

### What to copy from your class choice
* Hit Dice (how much HP you gain per level)
* Primary abilities (which stats matter most)
* Proficiencies (weapons, armor, tools, skills you are trained in)
* Saving throw proficiencies (which ability saves you are good at)
* Starting class features (the abilities you get at level 1)
* Class resource, if any (Rage, Ki, Spell slots, etc.)

### Why this matters
Your class affects almost every later number: HP growth, weapon choices, armor options, skill proficiency, class-specific resources.

### What to write down now
* class name
* Hit Dice
* proficiencies from class
* starting class features

## 7) Subclass is chosen later

Since all campaigns start at Level 1, subclass is not chosen yet.

The rule is that subclass is chosen at level 3.

### What to know about subclass
* At level 3, your class narrows into a specialization
* Examples: Wizard becomes Necromancer, Fighter becomes Tactician, Rogue becomes Assassin
* Think about which direction sounds interesting to you, but do not decide now

### What to write down now
* Just note that subclass comes at level 3
* Leave the subclass field blank on your sheet

## 8) Choose your background

Background explains who your character was before adventuring.

Background provides: skill proficiencies, tool proficiencies, starting items, roleplay hooks, and sometimes starting money.

### Available backgrounds with fixed bonuses

#### Soldier
* Skill Proficiencies: Athletics, Intimidation
* Tool Proficiency: One gaming set of your choice
* Starting Items: Military insignia, worn map, gaming set, common clothes, dice/cards
* Starting Money: 2 gp
* Backstory: You served in an army or militia. You are trained in combat and discipline.

#### Scholar
* Skill Proficiencies: History, Investigation
* Tool Proficiency: Calligrapher's Tools or Scholar's Kit
* Starting Items: Scholar's robes, ink and quill, book of lore, fine clothes, ink and paper
* Starting Money: 2 gp
* Backstory: You studied in a library, university, or monastery. You are trained in research and knowledge.

#### Criminal
* Skill Proficiencies: Stealth, Deception
* Tool Proficiency: Thieves' Tools
* Starting Items: Dark clothes, lock picks, deck of marked cards, crowbar, rope
* Starting Money: 3 gp
* Backstory: You lived outside the law. You are trained in stealth, theft, and deception.

#### Laborer
* Skill Proficiencies: Athletics, Animal Handling
* Tool Proficiency: One artisan's tool of your choice (Smith's Tools, Carpenter's Tools, etc.)
* Starting Items: Worn work clothes, calloused hands, tool of your trade, rope, common food
* Starting Money: 1 gp 5 sp
* Backstory: You worked a trade or labor job. You are strong and practical.

#### Merchant
* Skill Proficiencies: Insight, Deception
* Tool Proficiency: One gaming set of your choice
* Starting Items: Fine clothes, merchant's scale, ledger, 10 feet of rope, common clothes
* Starting Money: 4 gp
* Backstory: You bought and sold goods. You are trained in negotiation and reading people.

#### Acolyte
* Skill Proficiencies: Religion, Medicine
* Tool Proficiency: Herbalism Kit
* Starting Items: Holy symbol, prayer beads, religious texts, healing supplies, temple vestments
* Starting Money: 1 gp 5 sp
* Backstory: You served a religious institution. You are trained in faith and healing.

#### Folk Hero
* Skill Proficiencies: Survival, Animal Handling
* Tool Proficiency: One artisan's tool or musical instrument of your choice
* Starting Items: Clothes, musical instrument, trophy from your heroic deed, rope, common food
* Starting Money: 2 gp
* Backstory: You were a local champion. You are known and favored in your homeland.

#### Outcast
* Skill Proficiencies: Survival, Stealth
* Tool Proficiency: None
* Starting Items: Bedroll, tattered cloak, waterskin, rope, simple food rations
* Starting Money: 1 gp
* Backstory: You lived outside civilization. You are self-reliant and hardened.

### What to write down now
* background name
* skill proficiencies from background
* tool proficiency
* starting items
* starting money
* a one-sentence reason why you left home

## 9) Record all proficiencies

Proficiency means training. If your character is proficient, you add proficiency bonus when the rule applies.

### Proficiency categories
* Skills (like Athletics, Stealth, Insight)
* Weapons (which weapons you are trained with)
* Armor (which armor you can wear without penalty)
* Tools (which tools you are trained to use)

### Where proficiencies come from
* class gives weapon and armor proficiencies
* class and background both give skill proficiencies
* background gives tool proficiencies

### Write these down clearly
* all skill proficiencies
* all weapon proficiencies
* all armor proficiencies
* all tool proficiencies

## 10) Determine proficiency bonus

Your proficiency bonus is a global number that improves with level.

### Progression
* Level 1–4 → +2
* Level 5–8 → +3
* Level 9–12 → +4
* Level 13–16 → +5
* Level 17–20 → +6

**At Level 1, your proficiency bonus is +2.**

### Why it matters
This bonus is added to any roll where your character is proficient. It represents experience and training.

### What to note on the sheet
* Current level: 1
* Current proficiency bonus: +2

## 12) Calculate starting HP

Starting HP is calculated as follows:

**Starting HP = Hit Dice + Constitution Modifier**

### Examples
* Barbarian with d12 and +2 CON: 12 + 2 = 14 HP
* Wizard with d6 and +1 CON: 6 + 1 = 7 HP
* Cleric with d8 and +0 CON: 8 + 0 = 8 HP
* Fighter with d10 and +3 CON: 10 + 3 = 13 HP

**Minimum HP is always 1**, even if your Constitution modifier is negative.

### Write on your sheet
* Hit Dice: [your class's die, e.g., d10]
* Hit Points: [your calculated HP]
* Current HP: [same as above at level 1]

## 13) Calculate Armor Class (AC)

AC determines how hard you are to hit. It depends on your armor and sometimes Dexterity.

### AC calculations by armor type

#### No Armor
* AC = 10 + Dexterity modifier

#### Light Armor
* AC = 10 + Dexterity modifier + armor bonus
* Leather (light armor) typically gives +2 bonus
* You can add full Dexterity modifier

#### Medium Armor
* AC = Armor base AC + up to +2 from Dexterity modifier
* Leather or Chain Mail (medium armor) typically gives +5 or +6 base AC
* You can add up to +2 from Dexterity modifier
* Example: Chain Mail (AC 6) + 2 DEX = 8 AC

#### Heavy Armor
* AC = Armor base AC (Dexterity modifier does not apply)
* Plate armor typically gives +8 AC
* Strength 15 or higher is recommended to wear it without penalty

#### Shield
* Add +2 to your AC if you are holding a shield

### Example AC calculations
* Rogue in Leather Armor with 16 DEX: 10 + 3 (from DEX) + 2 (leather bonus) = 15 AC
* Fighter in Plate Armor: 8 AC (plate gives base 8; DEX does not apply)
* Monk with no armor, 16 DEX: 10 + 3 = 13 AC
* Cleric in Chain Mail with Shield and 10 DEX: 5 (chain mail base) + 0 (DEX) + 2 (shield) = 7 AC

### Write on your sheet
* Armor Class: [your calculated AC]

## 14) Calculate attack bonus and damage

For your main weapon, calculate the bonus you add to attack rolls and the damage you deal.

### Attack bonus
**Attack Roll = d20 + Ability Modifier + Proficiency Bonus (if proficient)**

Example: Barbarian with Strength 17 (+3 modifier), proficient with Great Sword:
* d20 + 3 (STR) + 2 (proficiency) = d20 + 5

### Damage
**Damage = [Weapon Damage Die] + Ability Modifier**

Example: Great Sword deals 2d6 damage:
* 2d6 + 3 (STR modifier) = 2d6 + 3

### Write on your sheet
* Main Weapon: [name]
* Damage Die: [e.g., 1d8, 2d6, 1d4]
* Damage Type: [e.g., slashing, piercing, bludgeoning]
* Attack Bonus: [d20 + X]
* Damage: [die + modifier]

## 15) Note spellcasting, if applicable

If your class casts spells, note the following:

### Spellcasting ability
Identify which ability is your spellcasting ability:
* Bard, Sorcerer, Warlock → Charisma
* Cleric, Druid, Monk (some features) → Wisdom
* Wizard → Intelligence

### Spell save DC
**Spell Save DC = 8 + Spellcasting Ability Modifier + Proficiency Bonus**

Example: Wizard with Intelligence 16 (+3 modifier) at Level 1:
* 8 + 3 (INT) + 2 (proficiency) = DC 13

### Spell attack bonus
**Spell Attack = d20 + Spellcasting Ability Modifier + Proficiency Bonus**

Example: Same Wizard making a spell attack:
* d20 + 3 (INT) + 2 (proficiency) = d20 + 5

### Write on your sheet
* Spellcasting Ability: [Intelligence/Wisdom/Charisma]
* Spell Save DC: [number]
* Spell Attack Bonus: d20 + [number]
* Number of Spell Slots (at level 1): [check your class]

## 16) Fill in character identity and personality

At this point the character is mechanically built, but still needs a lived-in identity.

### Add the following
* name
* appearance (height, build, hair, distinctive features)
* personality traits (3-5 words or phrases)
* motivations (what drives them forward?)
* goals (what do they want to achieve?)
* backstory (2-3 sentences about their life before adventuring)

### Helpful prompts
* What does the character look like?
* What made them leave home or take up adventuring?
* Who do they care about? Who do they dislike?
* What is one thing they really want?
* What is one flaw or insecurity they have?

## 17) Final review before play

Before the character is finished, do a full sheet check.

### Final checklist
- [ ] Race chosen and racial bonuses applied
- [ ] Ability scores assigned (before and after racial bonuses)
- [ ] Ability modifiers calculated
- [ ] Class chosen
- [ ] Hit Dice identified
- [ ] Starting HP calculated
- [ ] Languages recorded
- [ ] Background chosen
- [ ] Skill proficiencies recorded
- [ ] Tool proficiencies recorded
- [ ] Weapon proficiencies recorded
- [ ] Armor proficiencies recorded
- [ ] Proficiency bonus noted (+2 for level 1)
- [ ] Armor Class calculated
- [ ] Attack bonuses calculated for main weapon(s)
- [ ] Damage calculated for main weapon(s)
- [ ] Spell Save DC calculated (if applicable)
- [ ] Spell slots noted (if applicable)
- [ ] Starting equipment taken and listed
- [ ] Starting money spent or noted
- [ ] Name and personality written down
- [ ] Backstory written down

## 18) Finished character

If all of the above is complete, your character is ready for play.

The character should now feel complete in three ways:
* mechanically valid (all numbers correct)
* consistent with the rules (follows Point Buy, proficiencies, etc.)
* connected to a story concept (has personality and a reason to adventure)

You are ready for session one!
